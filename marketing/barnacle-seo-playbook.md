Title: What is DNFB, and how to clear a coding backlog

DNFB stands for Discharged Not Final Billed: the dollar value of completed
encounters that have been discharged but not yet finalized and billed, usually
because they are waiting to be coded. It is one of the least visible drains on a
practice's cash, because the revenue is earned - it is just stuck.

DNFB is often measured in "DNFB days" (DNFB dollars divided by average daily
revenue). A rising number almost always means one thing: a coding bottleneck.
Many organizations target under 5 DNFB days.

How to bring it down:

- Reconcile charges against the schedule so no rendered service goes uncoded.
- Give coders assistive suggestions (CPT, ICD-10, modifiers) and a tight
  feedback loop, so the same edits do not repeat.
- Clear the routine, compliant charts automatically and route only the complex
  or high-risk ones to a certified coder.
- Watch DNFB days as a daily KPI alongside days in A/R, not a monthly surprise.

The pattern that works is simple: automate the repetitive coding and charge
reconciliation, and keep humans on the judgment calls. That is how a backlog
stops being a recurring fire.

## 3. Reddit answers (r/medicalbilling, r/CodingandBilling, r/HealthIT)

Post these ONLY as genuine replies to relevant existing threads. Search each
subreddit for "denials", "DNFB", "prior auth", "backlog". Always add the

### For a "how do I reduce our denial rate?" thread

Most denials come from a handful of fixable sources, so the fastest win is to
measure by reason code first. Pull your denials by CARC/RARC for a month and you
will usually find eligibility, missing prior authorization, and coding/medical-
necessity account for the majority. Fixing those three root causes moves the
needle far more than working every claim individually.

Concretely:
- Verify eligibility at scheduling AND 24-48 hours before the visit (coverage
  changes at month boundaries).
- Keep a payer-specific list of what needs prior auth and confirm the auth
  number is on the claim before it goes out.
- Add a scrubbing step that flags diagnosis/procedure mismatches pre-submission.
- Work denials daily (appeal windows are tight) and log the root cause so you can
  prevent the next one.

Disclosure: I work on an RCM automation product, so take that lens for what it is.
If it helps, this free calculator benchmarks your denial rate against industry
numbers: https://medxflow.ai/denial-rate-calculator/

### For a "what does DNFB mean / how do I lower it?" thread

DNFB = Discharged Not Final Billed: completed encounters that are discharged but
not yet coded and billed, so the cash is earned but stuck. It is usually measured
in DNFB days (DNFB dollars / average daily revenue); under 5 days is a common
target. A rising DNFB almost always points to a coding bottleneck. The fix is
reconciling charges against the schedule, giving coders assistive suggestions, and
clearing routine charts fast so only complex ones need a human.
